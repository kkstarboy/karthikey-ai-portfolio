Multiple Concept Approaches — Smart Expense Tracker

Evidence basis and limitations

  • Decision: Which materially different interaction structures should advance to low-fidelity testing for spending comprehension and category recovery?
  • Scope: Mobile Smart Expense Tracker; discovery only.
  • Sources: Approved Phase 03 needs/problems and priorities; approved Phase 04 content, IA, study plan, sitemap, and flows; starter BRD.
  • User evidence: None. Candidate needs, journeys, labels, and paths remain LOW.
  • Missing: Direct research, analytics, tested IA, product owner, engineering estimates, data/privacy/security decisions, accessibility testing, current-app constraints, and approved release scope.
  • Confidence: All concepts and selection recommendations are Inferred/LOW. They are hypotheses, not validated preferences or commitments.

1. Concept brief

Problem

Provisional users may need a low-effort way to understand a spending period and recover from inaccurate categorization without complexity, judgment, or opaque financial-data behavior (Phase 03 N-01–N-06; PS-01–PS-04).

Non-negotiable risk constraints

  • Do not expose financial data without approved authentication/authorization and privacy controls.
  • Do not present stale, partial, inferred, or low-confidence data as certain.
  • Preserve correction, cancellation, error recovery, accessible alternatives, and user-controlled notifications.
  • Use calm, clear, non-judgmental language; do not optimize engagement at the expense of trust or harm.
  • Treat IA, user needs, and desired flows as provisional.

Dimensions varied

Primary navigation, entry point, information disclosure, user effort, automation visibility, and relationship to the existing transaction experience.

2. Multiple distinct approaches

Concept C-01 — Overview First

  • Core idea: A dedicated spending overview leads with period total, category distribution, comparison, and a small number of explainable insights.
  • User value hypothesis: Makes the full period legible at a glance and supports drill-down/recovery.
  • Interaction model: Dashboard → category detail → transaction detail/correction; peer destinations for budgets and recurring items.
  • Assumptions: People want a broad summary before a guided next step; categories are meaningful; information density remains manageable.
  • Strengths: Directly covers FR1/FR5; strong status-at-a-glance; supports multiple routes; correction can remain contextual.
  • Risks/limitations: Dense overview, chart dependence, ambiguous “insights,” potential information overload, totals may appear authoritative when data is partial.
  • Accessibility/trust: Text equivalent for visual summaries; data freshness and scope visible; no color-only encoding; explain calculations/categories.
  • Feasibility: Unknown. Depends on aggregation, categorization, comparison, insight, performance, and parent-app navigation.
  • Questions to test: Can people explain the period/category meaning? Which item is primary? Can they find correction, recurring items, and privacy information? Does the layout overwhelm new planners?
  • Links: N-01/N-02/N-06; F-01/F-02; sitemap N-11/N-112/N-114; BRD FR1–6.
  • Confidence/status: LOW / Retain for testing.

Concept C-02 — Guided Review

  • Core idea: A short, user-controlled review sequence answers one question at a time: total → largest/changed category → verify questionable items → choose or decline a next step.
  • User value hypothesis: Progressive disclosure may reduce cognitive load and make the experience easier for people new to planning.
  • Interaction model: Guided cards/steps with “skip,” “show full summary,” and “stop” controls; full detail remains available.
  • Assumptions: A sequential review is more comprehensible than a dense dashboard; the system can select safe, relevant steps without overclaiming intent.
  • Strengths: Focused hierarchy; explicit explanation; natural place for privacy/context; supports content testing.
  • Risks/limitations: Slower for repeat/expert users; automation may feel patronizing; ordering/relevance rules are unknown; progress framing may pressure completion.
  • Accessibility/trust: Clear step context, no forced completion, keyboard/screen-reader sequence, preserve back/skip, avoid emotional manipulation.
  • Feasibility: Unknown. Depends on eligibility/ordering logic, state persistence, explanation quality, and alternate route to full information.
  • Questions to test: Does guidance improve comprehension without reducing control? Do users understand why a step appears? Can they skip/exit and find full details?
  • Links: N-04/N-06; J-03; F-01/F-06; BRD FR1/5–8.
  • Confidence/status: LOW / Retain for testing.

Concept C-03 — Transaction Context

  • Core idea: Spending understanding and correction are layered onto the existing transaction activity, with category, recurring, and pattern context attached to familiar transaction/merchant records; aggregate summary is secondary.
  • User value hypothesis: Keeps explanations and correction close to the source transaction and may reduce navigation/context switching.
  • Interaction model: Existing activity/transaction → category/context actions → aggregate category/period links; contextual prompts rather than a feature-first dashboard.
  • Assumptions: Existing activity is a familiar, discoverable entry; the parent app supports extensions without duplication; users begin with a transaction rather than a monthly question.
  • Strengths: Strong traceability between total and source; contextual category recovery; potentially incremental implementation.
  • Risks/limitations: Existing app not supplied; broader patterns may be hard to see; high risk of duplicating transaction detail; prompts could clutter or expose sensitive context.
  • Accessibility/trust: Consistent labels with existing activity; explicit category source; accessible contextual actions; prevent unwanted financial insight disclosure.
  • Feasibility: Unknown and highly dependent on the current transaction architecture and ownership.
  • Questions to test: Is transaction activity the expected entry? Can people build a period-level understanding? Does contextual detail improve trust or increase noise?
  • Links: N-02/PS-04; F-02; sitemap OD-04; BRD FR2–4.
  • Confidence/status: LOW / Retain as a contrasting concept, pending parent-app access.

Concept C-04 — Commitment Calendar

  • Core idea: Organize recurring and budget-related content around upcoming and completed commitments rather than category totals.
  • User value hypothesis: May better support household commitment review than a general spending dashboard.
  • Interaction model: Timeline/calendar of regular commitments → item detail → category/period summary.
  • Assumptions: Timing is more decision-relevant than categories for household contexts; recurrence detection is reliable enough.
  • Strengths: Materially different focus; exposes fixed obligations; supports J-02.
  • Risks/limitations: Narrower audience, detection/privacy/shared-account uncertainty, calendar accessibility/complexity, cash-flow inference not specified.
  • Accessibility/trust: Provide list alternative; explain detected vs confirmed status; protect sensitive merchants/shared information.
  • Feasibility: Unknown; depends on reliable recurrence and permission logic.
  • Questions to test: Is time-based grouping useful? Which commitments belong? Does detected status cause false certainty?
  • Links: N-03; J-02; F-04; BRD FR9.
  • Confidence/status: LOW / Defer as focused variant unless household evidence increases.

6. Recommended concepts for testing

  1. Retain C-01 and C-02 as deliberately contrasting approaches for the same comprehension problem.
  2. Retain C-03 for a narrow correction/entry-point study only after existing-app architecture is available.
  3. Defer C-04 as a focused household variant; do not discard it. Re-enter if direct evidence supports time/commitment-centered behavior.
  4. Test comprehension, control, findability, error recovery, accessibility, privacy explanation, and emotional safety—not visual preference alone.

No winning concept is selected. G05 review may approve low-fidelity exploration but cannot substitute for representative-user testing or feasibility review.
