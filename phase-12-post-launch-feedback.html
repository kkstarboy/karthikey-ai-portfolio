7. Response-bias limitations

  • No respondents or sampling frame exist, so response rate, non-response, coverage, attrition, polarization, duplicate, and representativeness cannot be assessed.
  • Future in-product prompts may exclude people who cannot start or finish a task, have uninstalled, lack access, decline tracking, or use unsupported devices or assistive technology.
  • Interviews and opt-in studies may overrepresent people with time, access, motivation, trust, or strong experiences.
  • Support and public channels overrepresent help-seeking or vocal users and may contain duplicates, campaigns, unverifiable identity, and incomplete task context.
  • Self-report is Reported evidence and must not be silently promoted to observed behavior; analytics is not evidence of intent and cannot validate a quote’s meaning.
  • Leading satisfaction prompts, selective positive outreach, and promises of changes are prohibited. Negative, positive, null, conflicting, and exception evidence must remain visible.
  • No unnecessary personal, financial, account, transaction, authentication, health, disability, or demographic data may be collected. Consent, storage, access, retention, deletion, and escalation rules require approval.
