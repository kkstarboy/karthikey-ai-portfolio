1. Method decision

  • Selected mode: Moderated formative usability testing, proposed after readiness blockers are resolved.
  • Questions supported: Comprehension of period/total/data status; category correction and recovery; control and tone; permission/privacy understanding; selected concept comparison; accessibility barriers.
  • Why this mode fits: Prototype immaturity, missing/error states, sensitive financial context, need to distinguish prototype failure from usability friction, and need for careful probing/access accommodations.
  • Why unmoderated is not selected now: Non-functional controls, missing critical flows, ambiguous simulated success, and financial/privacy risks make unsupervised interpretation unsafe and data quality weak.
  • Risks: Facilitator influence, disclosure burden, participant distress, prototype failures, collection of unnecessary personal financial information, inaccessible tooling.
  • Mitigations: Neutral script, observe before probing, fictional scenarios, no account/financial details, explicit simulation disclosure, stop/skip rights, accessibility accommodations, pilot, predefined exclusions.
  • Quality controls: Version/hash check, facilitator training, note schema, task-order plan, prototype defect log, standardized completion/recovery definitions, member safety protocol.
  • Accessibility/privacy: Accessible consent/materials/tool; accommodation choice; minimize recording; pseudonymous IDs; no sensitive transaction/account data; approved retention/deletion.
  • Known limitations: Candidate segments are provisional; qualitative sessions cannot estimate prevalence; C-01/C-02 comparison requires equivalent prototype coverage.
  • Evidence/confidence: Method rationale Inferred/MEDIUM; execution readiness HIGH confidence as blocked by Phase 07 evidence.

Post-task questions

  • What did you expect to happen? What indicates the current state?
  • What information, if any, feels unclear or too certain?
  • What would you do if this information looked wrong?
  • What personal/data-use control would you expect here?

Do not ask leading preference questions such as “Was this easy?” before behavior/context probing.

5. Quality controls

  • Pilot the prototype, task wording, timing, facilitator behavior, recording, accessibility, and support path.
  • Hash/version the prototype and record any mid-study change; do not silently mix versions.
  • Define Success/Partial/Failure, error, hesitation, recovery, assistance, abandonment, and prototype failure before analysis.
  • Use a neutral facilitator guide; record every intervention.
  • Keep moderated and any later unmoderated results separate unless methods/metrics justify comparison.
  • Predefine session exclusion: consent failure, unusable recording where necessary, participant mismatch, severe prototype/tool failure, duplicate/fraud evidence; never exclude negative results because they are inconvenient.
  • Preserve severe accessibility/safety cases even when unique.

6. Analysis plan

  1. Standardize observations by participant ID, task, screen/flow, time, evidence type, and prototype version.
  2. Separate observed behavior, participant statement, facilitator intervention, prototype defect, researcher interpretation, and recommendation.
  3. Code navigation, comprehension, content, interaction, trust/privacy, error/recovery, accessibility, performance, and workflow friction.
  4. Record outcome, errors, hesitation, recovery, assistance, consequence, and context per task.
  5. Apply severity only after consequence/recovery and evidence review; preserve outliers/contradictions.
  6. Report sample counts, not prevalence percentages for small qualitative samples.
  7. Link each finding to participant/task evidence and prototype limitation; route high-risk findings to authorized owners.

7. Limitations

  • Prototype is not ready and critical paths are missing; no sessions may start from this plan yet.
  • No research owner, participant profile approval, sample rationale, tool, budget, timeline, consent, compensation, privacy, retention, or accessibility logistics are authorized.
  • Candidate user contexts are not validated recruitment segments.
  • A moderated study can identify barriers and mental models in context but cannot estimate market prevalence or causal business impact.
  • This plan does not establish that any task, metric, severity, or method has been approved.
